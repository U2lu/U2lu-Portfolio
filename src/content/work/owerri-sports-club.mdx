## The job

Owerri Sports Club, a sports club in Owerri. Fliers and posters for their social events — the pieces that tell members what is happening, when, and why to be there.

## Where it ran

Printed and posted locally in Owerri, plus the social posts that carried the same pieces.

## A recurring format

A club does not need one poster. It needs a series, and the series is the harder design problem.

Each event is different: a different date, a different reason to gather, sometimes a different audience inside the same club. If every piece is designed from nothing, the club stops looking like a club — it looks like a series of unrelated parties that happen to share a logo.

So the work is a system with one variable part. The constant has to be strong enough that the club is recognised before the words are read; the variable part has to be obvious enough that a member knows at a glance this is a new event and not last month's flier again.

## Designed to be re-skinned

The practical test of club collateral is whether the next piece can be made without the person who made the first one.

That means fixed margins, a small number of type sizes doing all the work, and detail kept away from the edges — because the same file will be cropped square for a feed, tall for a story, and printed at A2 without anyone re-drawing it. A layout that only works at one aspect ratio is a layout that gets rebuilt every month, badly.

## Small canvases

Most of these are seen on a phone. The hierarchy has to be brutal: what, when, where, and one image doing the emotional work. Everything else is a second look, and most people do not give it one.
